About Me

From Green Chemistry
to Targeted Oncology

I am a biochemistry researcher and MPhil candidate at Government College University Faisalabad (GCUF), Pakistan, where I have maintained a CGPA of 3.57/4.0. My research is situated at the convergence of nanomedicine, green chemistry, and cancer biology — disciplines I believe hold transformative potential for next-generation precision oncology.

My thesis investigates the in vivo anticancer efficacy of plant-mediated green-synthesized metal nanoparticles (MNPs) in murine tumor models, encompassing the full translational pipeline: from phytochemical synthesis and physicochemical characterization (SEM, XRD, EDX, Zetasizer) through to tumor suppression quantification, hematological profiling, and histopathological analysis of apoptotic markers.

Alongside my thesis, I have contributed to published research on Bacillus subtilis-mediated microbial nanofactories for silver nanoparticle biosynthesis, and on bioinformatics-driven molecular docking for estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer targets. These projects reflect my commitment to interdisciplinary, translational research.

I am actively seeking a fully funded PhD position in cancer nanomedicine, translational oncology, or biomedical nanotechnology at an internationally leading research institution, where I can develop targeted, plant-derived therapeutic platforms and contribute meaningfully to global cancer research.
